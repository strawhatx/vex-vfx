**Short answer:** promote a side project in a focused launch week. Post a short demo video on X and LinkedIn, launch on Product Hunt mid-week, share it where your users already hang out (the right subreddit or community, following its rules), and keep posting what you ship every week after. A 15–30 second video makes every one of those posts work harder.

## Before launch week

- **Know the one sentence.** What does it do, for whom? If you can't say it in under 12 words, your posts won't either.
- **Collect five screenshots** of the screens that make people want it.
- **Make the video** from those screenshots: a [16:9 cut for Product Hunt](/for/product-hunt-launch-video) and a [9:16 cut for social](/for/tiktok-app-promo). With [PromoReel](/create) that's a few minutes, free.
- **Set up a way to hear back:** a waitlist, an email field or a Discord link.

## A launch week that works

| Day       | What to do                                                                                                                         |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Monday    | Post the video on X and LinkedIn: one line on what it does, the video, the link.                                                   |
| Tuesday   | Write a short "why I built this" post. Reply to everyone.                                                                          |
| Wednesday | Launch on Product Hunt (12:01 a.m. Pacific). Put the video first in the gallery.                                                   |
| Thursday  | Share on the one or two communities your users read, following each one's self-promotion rules. Lead with the story, not the link. |
| Friday    | Post what you learned and what you're changing. People love a build-in-public update.                                              |

## Why a video helps

Text posts about software all look the same in a feed. A short video shows that the thing exists, looks good and works, in the time it takes to scroll past. It autoplays muted on X, LinkedIn and TikTok, so the captions do the talking.

You don't need to be on camera or know video editing. Screenshots in a good style, a hook in the first second and your URL on the last frame cover it. If your audience is developers, the [Retro Terminal](/aesthetics/retro-terminal) style reads as "made for you"; for consumer apps, [Y2K Chrome](/aesthetics/y2k-chrome) stops the scroll.

## After launch week: keep shipping in public

Most side projects don't fail at launch; they go quiet after it. Pick a day and post a [weekly "what I shipped" video](/for/what-i-shipped-video): three screenshots of what changed, a caption each. Same style every week so people recognize it. It's the cheapest marketing you'll ever do, and it compounds.
